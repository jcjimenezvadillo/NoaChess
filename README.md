## UCI Chess Engine in C# (.NET 10)

NoaChess is a modular UCI chess engine written from scratch in C# on .NET 10, with its own WPF board GUI. It follows an incremental, measured development process: every version is validated with unit tests, Perft, automated engine matches and Elo estimation before moving on. Development is AI-assisted; the architecture, the one-term-per-SPRT methodology, the calibrated gauntlets and every accept/cut decision are the author's own.

**v5.0.2.1 - in a lost position the tablebase filter left one move, and it was the wrong one.** Reported from a real bot game: the white king stood next to an undefended rook, and the engine walked away from it. `FilterRootMovesByTablebase` ranks root moves by DTZ and keeps a slack band around the best - but that band is zero unless the position is WON, so a lost root kept exactly one move. DTZ is the distance to the next irreversible move, so maximising it while losing means refusing to capture. Same binary, only the option changed: with tablebases `Kc6` and mate in 6, without them `Kxe8` and mate in 8. A plainly lost root is now not filtered at all; `BlessedLoss` keeps the filter where the fifty-move rule can still save the game. `SyzygyProbeLimit` goes back to 7, withdrawing v5.0.3: the 4.4x probe cost that justified dropping it was measured on this machine's mechanical drive, and the bot's tables are on an SSD where that cost does not exist.

**v5.0.0 - the desktop board stops being a test harness.** The Windows GUI has been rebuilt: pieces are **dragged**, moves can be **premoved** while the engine thinks, the game is written out in **figurine notation** and can be walked with first/back/forward/last, and the engine's search is shown as a **table of iterations** with depth, score, time, nodes and the principal variation in real notation, beside an evaluation bar that tracks winning chances. A **New Game dialog** picks the side and the time control, including a **real chess clock** with increment and losing on time; a **board editor** sets up any position; games are **saved and loaded as PGN**; a **whole-game review** marks every inaccuracy, mistake and blunder with an accuracy score; and while analysing, **every legal move is ranked** and the best few are drawn on the board as arrows on a green-to-orange scale. It also finds **where the game was decided** - the positions where the choice was worth something, which is not the same as the moves that were wrong, and which no other chess program reports. **Practise** replays the positions you got wrong and judges your second attempt against the move you actually played, and a PGN collection opens through a game picker rather than silently loading the first game. The **opening is named** above the move list and saved into the PGN as the ECO and Opening tags, matched by position so transpositions come out right. The engine can also be **held back to a level you can beat** (a node cap, described honestly as a cap rather than as a rating), it **varies its opening** so no two games are the same, and Ctrl+H asks it for a hint. **Any UCI engine on the machine can be loaded and given a colour**, and setting both colours to an engine starts a game that plays itself, with the evaluation bar and the search table following whichever engine is thinking. **The space bar plays the move for whoever is to move**, both sides included, so a set-up ending can be walked one press at a time, and the arrows, Home and End walk the game wherever the focus happens to be. The material beside each player shows only the **surplus** rather than every piece taken, with a light halo so the dark pieces are visible on the dark panel, and the move list **stays where you left it** when you click a move in it. Also replay, the board as an image, and a window that remembers where it was. Syzygy tablebases are a setting. Board colour schemes, coordinates, captured material, and settings that persist. **The engine is untouched**, so nothing here is measurable in Elo; the version constant is shared with the UCI `id name`, so the engine reports 5.0.0 without a search change behind it. Seven defects were found building it, none of them by reading the code: **two searches could run in the same engine at once** (the GUI's own fault, not the engine's, which documents that it is not allowed), engine progress could reach the bound panels **on the wrong thread**, **closing the window while the engine was thinking threw** because the gate serialising its searches was disposed underneath one, pausing a two-engine game silenced only the built-in engine, a move refused as illegal did not actually stop the game, the opening name handed out **null strings** that crashed the PGN of any game set up from a position, and in analysis mode **the evaluation waited on a full ranking of every legal move** before it was allowed to start, so the panel sat empty for 2727 ms after each move; the ordering is inverted and the evaluation now appears in 71 ms. `San.Format` joins `San.TryParse` in the Core so a move list has a writer, with 20 tests. 354 tests. See [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md).

**v4.6.2 - the training pipeline was the bottleneck, not the search: measured 3271 �40 CCRL, +128 over v4.5.0.** Feature factorization adds 704 virtual (piece, square) features during training and folds them **exactly** into the 22,528 real rows at export, so the engine, the file format and the arithmetic are untouched: **+195.4 �57.5 Elo**. It was found by measuring, not by guessing - the shipping net had **85.6% of its feature transformer quantising to zero** and 2,221 dead features, and stage-by-stage attribution put essentially the whole 38.5 cp quantisation error in the transformer rather than the head. Three engine fixes ship with it. **The Lazy SMP vote could be overruled by a blind worker**: `SearchResult` carried no depth, so a helper at depth 1 outvoted the main worker at depth 12 by 656 votes to 14, which played **two mates in one in four minutes** on Lichess while every `info` line printed the correct move - the PV comes from the main worker and the move came from the vote. **Helper threads are now a persistent pool** instead of being created per search, which took the per-move cost at 24 threads from 34.1 ms to 2.2 ms and flattened the curve. **A ponderhit whose relaunch comes back five or more plies shallower and disagrees now keeps the pondered move**, after one case in a hundred had a depth-1 answer overruling a depth-43 one on the same position. Also implemented and verified but not yet measured in games: quantization-aware training, which makes the trained net and the engine's integer arithmetic agree to **0.47 cp** instead of 31. See [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md).

**Previously: v4.5.0 - the same search, about 10% faster, with every step proved by byte-identical node counts.** The NNUE accumulator was **eager**: it copied both perspectives and did the feature math on every `MakeMove`, whether or not the position was ever evaluated - and v4.4.0 made that worse, because quiescence now reaches the transposition table and far more children return without ever calling the evaluator. A push now only records what the update would be; the accumulator is materialised on demand, walking back to the nearest computed ancestor. The first cut of that was **slower than what it replaced** while passing every test with byte-identical node counts, because collapsing the chain onto the top level leaves the intermediate levels uncomputed and a parent that never evaluates has its update replayed by every child; the counters added for it showed 5.6% *more* feature work than before. Separately, the engine's hot tables were declared as **multi-dimensional arrays** - history, killers, piece bitboards, Zobrist keys, counter moves, the LMR table - and .NET cannot give `T[,]` the single-dimension fast path: each access bounds-checks every rank and the JIT will not hoist that out of an inner loop. Flattening them is the largest single win in the release at **+6.12%**, 397 of 439 positions faster. Two measurement tools came out of it: `bench_time.py`, because comparing bench totals measures the machine rather than the change (two passes of the *same* binary differed 4.2%), and `clock_curve.py`, which **refuted** the hypothesis that time management was front-loading pathologically - the field spends 73% of its clock by move 25 against this engine's 78%. See [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md).

**v4.1.0 - BLOCK 12 data scale (infrastructure; strength unchanged, net still gen7).** The pipeline for a 300-500M position corpus. Feature decoding was the wall - 13,816 records/s meant **6 hours for 300M positions**, paid again on every change to the data mix; vectorised it runs at **169,366 records/s (12�, 29 minutes)**, asserted equal to the scalar reference over 50,000 real records. **v4.0.0 - BLOCK 12 foundation (no strength change).** The cost model that governed NNUE design was measured for the first time and it was wrong: the L1 dot product is **26.2%** of NNUE work, feature-transformer row traffic is **73.8%** - while the code had asserted for two versions that the dot product was *"THE cost of NNUE eval"*, which is precisely what justified keeping the network narrow. Ships the int8 L1 architecture (evaluation -25%), a king-square accumulator cache (refreshes 40-52� cheaper, 99.6% served), a streaming dataset that removes the 120M-record RAM ceiling, and a provenance gate that turns the v3.2.0 failure into a machine check. The embedded net is unchanged and a fixed-depth suite searches the same 193,746 nodes as before. Also fixed a pre-existing bug that saved checkpoints with no weights whenever the validation split was smaller than one batch. See [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md).

> **Active campaign: v4.x - NNUE architecture overhaul (branch `4.0.0`).** Five consecutive NNUE generations landed flat, and the cause has been identified: the network is **8× narrower** than reference-class nets (FT 128 vs 1024, one output bucket vs eight) and trained on roughly **100× less data** (13 M positions per generation against billions). The campaign runs **foundation → data → capacity → search**: int8 L1 quantization, an accumulator cache and a streaming training pipeline first; then a 300-500 M position dataset; then width and output buckets; then the search bundle. Expected destination ~3300-3450 CCRL. Full plan in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) (BLOCK 12).

### **Main Features**
- Bitboard board representation with **magic bitboards** for sliding pieces (**PEXT/BMI2** lookup on CPUs where it is fast - Intel and AMD Zen3+ - with an automatic CPUID guard).
- Full legal move generation (castling, en passant, promotions), validated against the public Perft reference values.
- **Search**: iterative deepening, aspiration windows with progressive widening, PVS, transposition table (Zobrist), **staged move generation with lazy legality** (TT move served without generating, then captures, then quiets), quiescence search, null-move pruning, singular extensions, SEE (static exchange evaluation) ordering and pruning, killer/history/counter-move/continuation-history ordering, history-informed logarithmic LMR, reverse futility pruning, futility pruning, late move pruning, Internal Iterative Reductions, ProbCut.
- **Parallel search (Lazy SMP)**: up to 32 worker threads search the same root sharing one lock-free transposition table (benign races, key-verified) and vote on the move; the board, search stack, histories and evaluator accumulators are per-thread. `Threads=1` is byte-identical to the single-threaded search. An SMP-only time-management guard (instability averaged over the pool, soft deadline capped, node-level mid-iteration cap) bounds a ponderhit clock spike.
- **Evaluation**: tapered (middlegame/endgame) evaluation blended by game phase - PeSTO piece values, two-phase piece-square tables, reference-style attack bitboards (attackedBy per piece type + double attacks) feeding a full threat evaluation (hanging pieces, threats by minor/rook/king, safe-pawn and pawn-push threats, restricted pieces, knight/slider threats on the queen), king safety (attack units + pawn shield through quadratic danger curve), non-linear piece mobility (reference lookup tables over a reference-style mobility area, with x-ray attacks and pinned-piece restriction), tempo bonus, knight outposts, space, bishop pair, rooks on open/semi-open files and on the 7th rank, tapered pawn structure (doubled/isolated/passed/backward, phalanx, connected passers, blocked passers, rook behind passer); mop-up term for won endgames.
- **NNUE evaluation**: HalfKAv2_hm neural network (InputSize 22528, kings as features), quantized integer weights, incremental accumulators, AVX2 SIMD inference; trained from self-play datagen with WDL mixing and resign/draw adjudication, refined by generational self-play. Beats the classical evaluator; selectable via UCI `UseNNUE` / `EvalFile` options, with the current net embedded in the executable.
- **Time management**: soft/hard budgets, 85% increment use, conservative ply-adaptive horizon, movestogo support, GUI-latency safety margins.
- **Full UCI**: uci, isready, ucinewgame, setoption (Hash, Threads, MoveOverhead, Profile, UseNNUE, EvalFile), position, go (depth/nodes/movetime/clock/ponder/infinite), stop, quit. Asynchronous search; publishes as a single self-contained .exe.
- Engine profiles (Default/Bullet) with tunable search/time parameters.
- **WPF GUI** (MVVM): drag-and-drop board with vector piece graphics (Cburnett SVG), external UCI engines on either colour and engine-versus-engine games, premoves including recaptures, first/back/forward/last navigation, a figurine move list, an Arena-style table of search iterations with the principal variation in real notation, an evaluation bar, real chess clocks with increment, a board editor, PGN open/save/copy/paste, whole-game review with blunder annotations and accuracy, a ranked list of every legal move with colour-scaled candidate arrows on the board, Syzygy tablebase support, board colour schemes, captured material, and automatic NNUE loading. Play as either colour or analyse both sides.
- Allocation-free hot paths; BenchmarkDotNet benchmark suite.

### **Solution Layout**
```
src/NoaChess.Core      -> pure chess rules: board, bitboards, moves, FEN, Zobrist
src/NoaChess.Engine    -> search, evaluation, heuristics, TT, time management
src/NoaChess.UCI       -> UCI console host (the engine executable for GUIs)
src/NoaChess.GUI.Wpf   -> WPF board application (MVVM)
tests/                 -> xUnit test suites (Perft, make/unmake, search, SEE...)
benchmarks/            -> BenchmarkDotNet micro-benchmarks
```
Dependency rule: `Core` depends on nothing; `Engine` uses `Core`; `UCI` and the GUI are thin hosts. The full plan lives in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

### **Build & Run**
```bash
dotnet test                                   # run the full test suite
dotnet run --project src/NoaChess.GUI.Wpf    # play against the engine (Windows)
dotnet publish src/NoaChess.UCI -c Release -o out   # single-file UCI engine .exe
```
The published `NoaChess.UCI.exe` works in any UCI GUI (Arena, CuteChess, BanksiaGUI...).

### **Roadmap & Versions** (newest first)
- **Threat features, measured 2026-08-23: +47.8 Elo [+21.6, +74.5] at fixed nodes over 345 games, H1 accepted** - the first net to beat the one the bots play since 10 August, after 60 epochs and 125 hours of training. **It cannot ship yet**: the net runs at 0.33x the NPS and gives up 1.6 plies at 3+2, so it enters a real game with about +48 of evaluation against -55 of speed. Half the search time is the threat machinery itself (CollectFrom 16.4%, the delta 19.9%, refresh 10.5%), which is the cost of tracking ~37 features per position rather than something a micro-optimisation removes. Two attempts to speed it up were measured: preallocated buffers instead of `stackalloc` (worth ~3%, not the 96% a sampling profile suggested - a profile says WHERE to look, only a stopwatch says how much it is worth) and a sorted merge instead of the linear scan, which measured WORSE and was reverted because 83% of features survive a move so the scan exits early while a sort pays for every element.
- **Architecture 5, measured 2026-08-23 and rejected: -32.8 Elo [-56.5, -9.3] over 575 games** - the head rebuilt with a pairwise transformer read, squared activations, a second hidden layer and a linear bypass. The likely cause is design rather than implementation: the pairwise read HALVES the L1 input, leaving 64 values per perspective, and the technique was copied without the width that sustains it. The parity checks were exact in both directions and could not have caught this - they verify that engine and trainer compute the same thing, not that the thing is good.
- **Measured 2026-08-24, and neither of these ships yet.** **Threats are closed: +47.8 Elo [+21.6, +74.5] at fixed nodes but -14.9 [-30.1, +0.1] at 180+2 over 723 games, H0**, steady across all seven blocks of 100. The evaluation is genuinely better and the speed cost eats it. **Width 512 is open: +38.4 Elo [+18.7, +58.3] at fixed nodes over 637 games, H1**, once trained to convergence - the -30.3 that had closed the width axis for weeks compared a brand cut at EPOCH 5 OF 60 against a converged one, and the width-256 net that seemed to confirm it turns out to have been cut at epoch 5 as well. **The byproduct outlasts both verdicts: crossing +47.8 at fixed nodes with -14.9 at the clock at 0.510x NPS calibrates that a doubling of speed is worth about 65 Elo in this engine at 180+2**, a constant previously borrowed and guessed anywhere between 0.58x and 0.70x. It turns every capacity question into a question about CHEAP capacity, and prices width 512 at 0.655x against a 0.664x break-even - exactly on the line, which is why its clock SPRT cannot be replaced by arithmetic.
- **v5.0.2.1** - **In a lost position the tablebase filter left one move, and it was the wrong one.** Reported from a real bot game with screenshots and reproduced on the position: `4r3/1P1K4/8/8/6p1/q6k/8/8 w`, where the king on d7 sits next to an undefended rook on e8 and `Kxe8` also clears b8 for the pawn. Same binary, only the option changed: **tablebases at limit 7 play `Kc6` and are mated in 6; with no tablebases `Kxe8` and mate in 8.** With the tables it is mated two moves sooner AND cannot see the alternative, because the root filter removed it. The band the filter keeps is `bestRank > MaxDtz / 2 ? ... : 0`, and that condition means "won" - so a lost root keeps exactly ONE move, and since DTZ is the distance to the next IRREVERSIBLE move, maximising it while losing means refusing to capture. Every line of that filter was written to protect a win. A plain loss is now not filtered; **`BlessedLoss` is what makes that safe**, because there the fifty-move rule genuinely can still save the game. ## Motor de ajedrez UCI en C# (.NET 10)

NoaChess es un motor de ajedrez UCI modular escrito desde cero en C# sobre .NET 10, con su propia GUI de tablero en WPF. Sigue un desarrollo incremental y medido: cada versión se valida con tests unitarios, Perft, matches automáticos entre motores y estimación de Elo antes de avanzar. El desarrollo está asistido por IA (Claude como herramienta de pair-programming); la arquitectura, la metodología de un término por SPRT, los gauntlets calibrados y cada decisión de aceptar o cortar un término son del autor.

**Versión actual: v5.0.2.1 - en posición perdida el filtro de tablas dejaba una sola jugada, y era la mala.** Detectado en una partida real del bot: el rey blanco estaba pegado a una torre sin defender y el motor se apartó de ella. `FilterRootMovesByTablebase` ordena las jugadas de raíz por DTZ y conserva un margen alrededor de la mejor, pero ese margen es cero salvo que la posición esté GANADA, así que una raíz perdida se quedaba con una única jugada. DTZ es la distancia al siguiente movimiento irreversible, de modo que maximizarlo mientras pierdes significa negarse a capturar. Mismo binario, solo cambia la opción: con tablas `Kc6` y mate en 6, sin ellas `Kxe8` y mate en 8. Una raíz simplemente perdida ya no se filtra; `BlessedLoss` mantiene el filtro donde la regla de 50 jugadas todavía puede salvar la partida. `SyzygyProbeLimit` vuelve a 7 y retira el cambio de v5.0.3: el coste de sondeo de 4,4x que lo justificaba se midió con las tablas en el disco mecánico de esta máquina, y las del bot están en un SSD donde ese coste no existe.

**v5.0.0 - el tablero de escritorio deja de ser un arnés de pruebas.** La GUI de Windows reescrita: arrastrar y soltar, premoves incluidas las recapturas, navegación y lista de jugadas con figurines, salida del motor como tabla de iteraciones con la variante en notación real y barra de evaluación sobre la curva de probabilidad de victoria. Diálogo de nueva partida con bando, nivel y control de tiempo; relojes de verdad; editor de posiciones; PGN de entrada y salida; revisión de la partida completa con precisión; todas las jugadas legales ordenadas con flechas de color; apertura nombrada por posición y guardada en el PGN; **motores UCI externos en cualquiera de los dos colores** y partidas entre dos de ellos; la barra espaciadora juega la jugada del bando al que le toca. **El motor no se toca, así que nada de esto es medible en Elo.** 354 tests. Ver [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md).

### **Características principales**
- Representación por bitboards con **magic bitboards** para piezas deslizantes (lookup **PEXT/BMI2** en CPUs donde es rápido - Intel y AMD Zen3+ - con guarda automática por CPUID).
- Generación de movimientos legales completa (enroques, al paso, promociones), validada contra los valores públicos de referencia de Perft.
- **Búsqueda**: iterative deepening, aspiration windows con ensanchado progresivo, PVS, tabla de transposición (Zobrist), **generación de movimientos por etapas con legalidad perezosa** (jugada de TT servida sin generar, luego capturas, luego silenciosas), quiescence, null-move pruning, singular extensions, SEE para ordenación y poda, ordenación por killers/history/counter-move/continuation history, LMR logarítmico informado por historia, reverse futility pruning, futility pruning, late move pruning, Internal Iterative Reductions, ProbCut.
- **Búsqueda paralela (Lazy SMP)**: hasta 32 hilos worker buscan la misma raíz compartiendo una sola tabla de transposición lock-free (carreras benignas, verificadas por clave) y votan la jugada; el tablero, la pila de búsqueda, las historias y los acumuladores del evaluador son por hilo. `Threads=1` es byte-idéntico a la búsqueda de un solo hilo. Un guard de gestión de tiempo solo-SMP (instabilidad promediada sobre el pool, deadline soft acotado, cap a nivel de nodo a media iteración) acota un pico de reloj en ponderhit.
- **Evaluación**: evaluación tapered (fase media/final) mezclada por fase de juego - valores PeSTO, tablas pieza-casilla de dos fases, bitboards de ataque al estilo de referencia (attackedBy por tipo de pieza + dobles ataques) que alimentan una evaluación de amenazas completa (piezas colgadas, amenazas de menor/torre/rey, amenazas de peón seguro y de avance de peón, piezas restringidas, amenazas de caballo/deslizantes sobre la dama), seguridad del rey (unidades de ataque + escudo de peones mediante curva cuadrática), movilidad no-lineal de piezas (tablas lookup de referencia sobre un área de movilidad al estilo de referencia, con ataques x-ray y restricción de piezas clavadas), bonus de tempo, outposts de caballo, espacio, par de alfiles, torres en columnas abiertas/semiabiertas y en séptima fila, estructura de peones tapered (doblados/aislados/pasados/retrasados, peones en falange, pasados conectados, pasados bloqueados, torre detrás del pasado); término mop-up para finales ganados.
- **Evaluación NNUE**: red neuronal HalfKAv2_hm (InputSize 22528, reyes como features), pesos enteros cuantizados, acumuladores incrementales, inferencia SIMD AVX2; entrenada con datagen de self-play con mezcla WDL y adjudicación por resign/tablas, refinada por self-play generacional. Supera al evaluador clásico; seleccionable con las opciones UCI `UseNNUE` / `EvalFile`, con la red actual embebida en el ejecutable.
- **Gestión de tiempo**: presupuestos soft/hard, uso del 85% del incremento, horizonte adaptativo conservador por ply, soporte movestogo, márgenes de seguridad para la latencia de la GUI.
- **UCI completo**: uci, isready, ucinewgame, setoption (Hash, Threads, MoveOverhead, Profile, UseNNUE, EvalFile), position, go (depth/nodes/movetime/reloj/ponder/infinite), stop, quit. Búsqueda asíncrona; se publica como .exe único autocontenido.
- Perfiles de motor (Default/Bullet) con parámetros de búsqueda/tiempo ajustables.
- **GUI WPF** (MVVM): tablero con arrastrar y soltar y piezas vectoriales (SVG Cburnett), motores UCI externos en cualquiera de los dos colores y partidas entre dos motores, premoves incluidas las recapturas, navegacion primera/atras/adelante/ultima, lista de jugadas con figurines, tabla de iteraciones de busqueda con la variante principal en notacion real, barra de evaluacion, relojes con incremento, editor de posiciones, PGN abrir/guardar/copiar/pegar, revision de la partida completa con anotaciones de error y precision, lista ordenada de todas las jugadas legales con flechas de color en el tablero, tablas Syzygy, esquemas de color, material capturado y carga automatica de la red NNUE. Juega con blancas, con negras o analiza ambos bandos.
- Hot paths sin allocations; suite de benchmarks con BenchmarkDotNet.
